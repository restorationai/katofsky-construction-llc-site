## What should a fire damage company in Pittsburgh actually handle?

A fire damage company worth hiring should be able to secure the structure, remove soot and odor, pack out and store your belongings, and rebuild what was lost, all without bringing in a second company. That single-contractor structure matters more after a fire than after almost any other kind of loss, because fire jobs touch structure, contents, and air quality all at once.

When the cleanup crew and the rebuild crew are two different businesses, somebody has to coordinate the handoff: confirming the structure is dry and clean enough to frame over, matching the insurance scope between two estimates, scheduling around two different crews. ## What certifications should you verify before hiring?

At minimum, verify the company is an IICRC Certified Firm and carries a current Pennsylvania home improvement contractor license. Those two facts tell you the crew is trained to a recognized industry standard and the business is accountable under state law.

Fire and smoke restoration work is supposed to follow the [ANSI/IICRC S700 Standard for Professional Fire and Smoke Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s700/), which covers everything from pre-cleaning assessment to soot removal technique by material type. Ask any company you're considering whether their technicians hold IICRC fire restoration training, not just a general cleaning credential. Katofsky Construction LLC is an [IICRC Certified Firm](https://www.iicrc.org/) and holds PA Home Improvement Contractor license #090877; you can confirm any Pennsylvania contractor's registration status through the [PA Attorney General's home improvement contractor lookup](https://hicsearch.attorneygeneral.gov/).

Also confirm the company carries its own liability insurance, separate from yours. A fire-damaged structure involves demolition, temporary power, and sometimes scaffolding; you want the contractor's insurance covering their own crew and equipment on site, not just a verbal assurance.

## How does the insurance claims process work for a fire loss in Pennsylvania?

Most homeowners policies cover sudden fire damage, including smoke and soot cleanup and the structural rebuild, but the payout depends on accurate documentation from day one. The company you hire should document damage before any cleanup starts and provide a written scope that matches what the adjuster is seeing.

Pennsylvania homeowners can review their coverage basics through the state's [consumer insurance guidance](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/insurance/consumer-help-center/learn-about-insurance.html), and the Insurance Information Institute has a good plain-language overview of [how claims typically get settled after a disaster](https://www.iii.org/article/settling-insurance-claims-after-a-disaster). What matters on your end: keep receipts for any temporary lodging, don't discard damaged contents before they're documented or inventoried, and ask your contractor for an itemized scope rather than a lump-sum estimate. A vague one-line invoice is one of the most common reasons claims stall.

## What questions should you ask before signing a contract?

Before you sign anything, ask these four questions and expect a direct answer to each:

- Does your crew handle the full job, from board-up through final rebuild, or do you subcontract the reconstruction?
- Can I see your IICRC certification and current PA home improvement contractor license number?
- Will you provide a written, itemized scope of work before demolition starts?
- How do you handle contents that need to be packed out, cleaned, and stored during the rebuild?

A company that hesitates on the license number or can't explain how contents storage works is worth a second look elsewhere. These aren't trick questions; they're the same things a careful adjuster will ask.
