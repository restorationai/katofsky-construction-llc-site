## What Do Roofing Services Actually Include?

Roofing services span four distinct jobs: inspection, repair, full replacement, and emergency tarping, and a quote should tell you which one you're buying. An inspection checks flashing, shingle condition, decking, and attic ventilation, usually for a flat fee or free with a repair estimate. A repair addresses a specific failure point, like a cracked boot around a vent pipe or a section of lifted shingles.

A full replacement tears off the existing roofing down to the decking, replaces damaged sheathing, and installs new underlayment, flashing, and shingles or another roofing material. Emergency tarping is a temporary, weather-tight cover placed after storm or wind damage to stop water intrusion until permanent repairs can be scheduled. [emergency board-up and tarping](/services/emergency-board-up-tarping/) is its own line item for a reason: it buys time without pretending to be the fix.

Most Pittsburgh-area roofs are asphalt shingle, with some older homes in neighborhoods like Fox Chapel still carrying original slate or standing-seam metal sections. The material on your roof changes both the repair technique and the cost, so an honest estimate names the material before it names a number.

## How Much Do Roofing Services Cost in Pittsburgh, PA?

Typical asphalt shingle replacement in the Pittsburgh area runs roughly $9,000 to $18,000 for an average single-family home, with wide swings based on roof size, pitch, and the number of layers being removed. Spot repairs usually fall between $350 and $1,200, and emergency tarping after storm damage is typically priced separately from the eventual repair.

Several factors move the number. Steep-pitch roofs and multi-story homes cost more in labor because of access and safety requirements. Full tear-offs with rotted decking cost more than a straightforward overlay, since damaged plywood has to be replaced before new material goes down. Metal or slate roofing, more common on older homes in areas like Mount Lebanon and Fox Chapel, costs significantly more per square than asphalt shingle, both to repair and to replace in kind.

| Job Type | Typical PA Range |
|---|---|
| Roof inspection | $0-$250 |
| Minor shingle/flashing repair | $350-$1,200 |
| Emergency tarping | $300-$900 |
| Full asphalt shingle replacement (avg. home) | $9,000-$18,000 |
| Metal or slate roof repair/replacement | $15,000-$40,000+ |

Homeowners insurance typically covers roof damage from a sudden, covered peril, like wind, hail, or a fallen tree limb, but not gradual wear or age-related failure. If a storm is involved, document the damage with photos before any tarping or repair begins; adjusters weigh pre-repair documentation heavily when evaluating a claim.

## What Should You Check Before Hiring a Roofing Contractor?

Verify the contractor's Pennsylvania Home Improvement Contractor (HIC) registration before signing anything, since unregistered roofing work is a common source of dropped warranties and unfinished jobs. You can look up any contractor's HIC status directly through the [Pennsylvania Office of Attorney General's HIC search](https://hicsearch.attorneygeneral.gov/).

Beyond registration, confirm the contractor carries general liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage, ask whether the warranty covers labor as well as materials, and get the scope of work in writing, including the specific underlayment, shingle brand, and flashing details. A contractor who won't put the material specs in writing is one you should be cautious about.

Storm chasers, crews that show up door-to-door after a hailstorm with pressure to sign same-day, are worth extra scrutiny. A legitimate local contractor will give you time to review the contract and will still be reachable the following season if a warranty issue comes up.
