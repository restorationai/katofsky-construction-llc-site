## How Do You Compare Air Duct Cleaning Companies in Pittsburgh?

The company you hire should use source-removal methods that meet industry standards, carry proof of licensing and insurance, and show you the actual condition of your ductwork rather than just telling you it's dirty. Those three things separate a legitimate cleaning from a quick "blow and go" visit.

Look for these specifics before booking:

- **Method**: negative-pressure containment with a HEPA-filtered vacuum collection system, combined with mechanical agitation (rotary brushes or air whips) to dislodge debris stuck to duct walls. The [National Air Duct Cleaners Association (NADCA)](https://nadca.com/) sets the industry standard most reputable companies follow.
- **Scope**: supply registers, return registers, trunk lines, the coil, and the blower wheel should all be included, not just the visible vents.
- **Documentation**: a company that shows you before-and-after photos or video of the inside of the ducts is giving you something to verify, not just a receipt.
- **Licensing and insurance**: confirm the company can produce a license number and current insurance certificate before work starts.

## What Happens During a Professional Duct Cleaning?

A professional duct cleaning follows a set sequence: inspect, contain, agitate, extract, and verify. Skipping any one of those steps is how a homeowner ends up with vents that look clean on the surface but still have debris packed in the trunk lines.

1. **Inspection**: a technician checks accessible duct runs, often with a camera, to assess buildup and confirm the system layout.
2. **Containment setup**: registers are sealed and a negative-pressure vacuum collection unit is connected to the system so loosened debris doesn't blow back into the living space.
3. **Agitation**: rotary brushes or compressed-air whips knock debris loose from the interior duct walls.
4. **HEPA extraction**: the negative-pressure system pulls dislodged material out of the ducts and into a sealed collection unit.
5. **Coil and blower cleaning**: the evaporator coil and blower wheel are cleaned, since buildup there restricts airflow and strains the system.
6. **Final check and reseal**: registers are reinstalled and the technician does a final visual pass before closing out the job.

Keeping registers and return air paths clear of debris buildup supports better [indoor air quality](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq) in homes with heavier dust loads, pets, or recent renovation work.
