## Is the Contractor Licensed to Handle Asbestos in Pennsylvania?

Ask for two things: the contractor's Pennsylvania home improvement contractor registration number, and confirmation that the crew performing the removal holds current asbestos worker and supervisor accreditation through the Pennsylvania Department of Labor and Industry. Both matter, and they are not the same credential.

General contractor registration (what most Pennsylvania homeowners can verify through the [PA Attorney General's Home Improvement Contractor registry](https://www.attorneygeneral.gov/businesses-and-organizations/home-improvement-contractor-registration/)) confirms the business is registered to do home improvement work in the state. Asbestos-specific accreditation is a separate layer administered by the state's Department of Labor and Industry, and it covers the individual workers and supervisors on site, not just the company name on the truck. ## What Happens During Air Clearance Testing?

Air clearance testing confirms fiber counts in the work area have dropped to an acceptable level before the containment barrier comes down and the space is cleared for normal use. A qualified contractor arranges for an independent, third-party lab to collect and analyze the samples, not their own crew.

The technician sets up a negative-air machine with HEPA filtration to pull air out of the containment zone during the work, then a separate sampling team collects air samples once the visible removal and cleanup is finished. Those samples go to an accredited lab for analysis under phase contrast microscopy or, for more precise readings, transmission electron microscopy. You should receive a written clearance report before the containment plastic comes down. For general background on fiber exposure and health guidance, the [EPA's asbestos resource page](https://www.epa.gov/asbestos) is a good plain-language reference.

## Where Does the Asbestos Waste Actually Go?

Asbestos waste must be double-bagged in labeled, leak-tight containers and hauled to a landfill permitted to accept it, with a documented waste manifest tracking the material from your property to final disposal. A contractor who can't describe this chain of custody shouldn't be in your basement.

Pennsylvania and federal rules under the EPA's hazardous air pollutant standards require notification before certain renovation or demolition projects involving asbestos, along with proper wetting of materials during removal to limit airborne fibers, approved transport, and disposal records you can request a copy of. If your project also turns up old painted trim or window sashes, many of these same pre-1980 properties need [lead paint abatement](/services/lead-paint-abatement/) addressed at the same time, since the two often show up together in homes of that era.

## What Should You Ask Before Signing a Contract?

Ask these questions before any contractor sets foot in your containment area:

- Can you provide your Pennsylvania contractor license number and asbestos accreditation documentation?
- Who performs the air clearance testing, and is that lab independent of your company?
- What does the written scope of work include, and what's excluded?
- How is the waste bagged, labeled, and tracked to disposal?
- What's the timeline from containment setup to clearance?
