## Who actually handles asbestos work: a tester, a contractor, or the county?

Three separate parties typically handle an asbestos project in Allegheny County: an accredited inspector or lab identifies the material, a licensed asbestos abatement contractor removes or encapsulates it, and the Allegheny County Health Department reviews the required notification before certain renovation or demolition work begins. Missing any one of these steps can leave a homeowner out of compliance even after the material is physically gone.

Testing comes first if the material hasn't already been identified. A trained sampling technician collects a small piece of the suspect material, bags it, and sends it to an accredited lab for analysis. Store-bought test kits don't carry the chain of custody that most contractors, insurers, or the county will accept, so a result from an accredited lab is worth the extra step before anyone starts cutting into a wall or floor.

Once a material comes back positive, removal goes to a contractor accredited for asbestos work under Pennsylvania's state program. That contractor sets up containment, runs the work under negative pressure, and disposes of the material at an approved facility. For older homes across Pittsburgh neighborhoods like Lawrenceville and the Mon Valley mill towns, this is usually where a renovation stalls until the material clears.

The county's role is permitting and notification, not removal. The Allegheny County Health Department administers its own asbestos regulation for work inside county limits, and certain renovation or demolition projects require advance notification before work starts, regardless of whether the home is occupied. We handle that filing as part of a project scope so homeowners don't have to track it down themselves.

## What happens when you call for asbestos removal?

The first call is mostly information gathering: how old the structure is, where the suspect material sits, and whether anything has already been tested. From there, scheduling moves straight to sampling if the material is still unknown, or straight to a written scope if a lab report is already in hand.

Have a few details ready before you call: the year the building went up (anything before 1980 is worth treating as suspect until tested), which material is involved (floor tile, popcorn ceiling, pipe or boiler insulation, siding, roofing felt), and roughly how large the affected area is. If you already have a lab result, keep that handy too.

On-site, a technician assesses the material, confirms what still needs sampling, and builds a written scope before any work begins. If the Allegheny County notification applies, we file it and schedule the project around the county's required waiting period rather than starting early and risking a stop-work order. Containment goes up, air monitoring runs during the work, and clearance testing confirms the area is safe before containment comes down.

## When does a Pittsburgh renovation need a county notification?

Most renovation or demolition work on a pre-1980 Pittsburgh-area structure needs an asbestos survey before the project starts, and projects that will disturb a regulated quantity of asbestos-containing material need advance notification filed with the Allegheny County Health Department before work begins. This applies to nearly all commercial demolitions and to renovations once the amount of disturbed material crosses the regulatory threshold.

The [EPA's asbestos program](https://www.epa.gov/asbestos) sets the federal baseline that local programs like Allegheny County's build on, covering work practices, disposal, and notification for regulated projects. Homeowners doing a small repair on a single, already-tested non-friable material sometimes fall outside the notification requirement, but the only way to know for certain is to test first and ask before work starts, not after a wall is already open.
