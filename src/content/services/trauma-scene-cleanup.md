## What Trauma Scene Cleanup actually involves

Trauma scene cleanup covers the removal and decontamination of biological material following a death, accident, or violent incident, along with the materials it has affected: flooring, subflooring, drywall, upholstery, and in some cases structural framing. The work is governed by OSHA bloodborne pathogen standards and EPA-registered disinfectant protocols, not general cleaning products. Depending on the scope, a response can run from a few hours for a contained area to multiple days when flooring or wall cavities need to be opened up. Our crews work in full PPE, use enzymatic and hospital-grade disinfecting agents, and document every step for the property owner and the insurance carrier. Katofsky Construction LLC is an IICRC Certified Firm, and our technicians follow established decontamination protocols rather than improvised cleaning methods.

## Our process

1. **Scene assessment and PPE staging.** Before any cleaning begins, our technician evaluates the affected area, confirms the scene has been released by law enforcement or the medical examiner, and stages the correct personal protective equipment for the exposure level.
2. **Bio-contaminant removal.** Visible biological material and saturated porous materials (carpet, padding, certain drywall sections) are removed and bagged according to regulated waste handling procedures. Non-porous surfaces are treated separately from porous ones because they respond differently to disinfection.
3. **Enzymatic and EPA-registered disinfection.** Affected surfaces are treated with hospital-grade, EPA-registered disinfectants and enzymatic cleaners designed to break down residual organic material at the molecular level, not just mask it.
4. **Odor neutralization.** Odor from trauma scenes often sits in subflooring or wall cavities rather than on the surface, so neutralization may require sealing or removal of affected substrate, not just surface deodorizing.
5. **Waste manifest and disposal.** All regulated waste is logged, packaged, and transported for disposal in compliance with state and federal biomedical waste regulations, with documentation provided to the property owner for insurance and legal records.

## What separates a good trauma scene cleanup response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating a trauma scene like a deep-cleaning job. Surface disinfection without removing saturated porous materials leaves contamination and odor behind, sometimes for months, because blood and bodily fluids wick into subflooring, grout lines, and wall cavities far beyond the visible stain. A less experienced crew may also skip proper waste manifest documentation, which creates problems later if a landlord, estate, or insurance adjuster asks for proof the job was done to code. Adjusters reviewing a trauma cleanup claim typically look for itemized material removal, disposal records, and before-and-after documentation, not just an invoice that says "cleaning service." A thorough response treats each surface type on its own terms and documents the decision to remove material versus disinfect it in place.

Pittsburgh's housing stock, much of it pre-1950s construction with original hardwood flooring, plaster walls, and unsealed subfloors, tends to be more porous than newer builds, which means contamination can travel further into building materials than it would in a modern home with engineered flooring and sealed drywall.
