**Fire damage in Homestead doesn't stay contained to one room.** Smoke travels through century-old plaster walls and duct cavities built for coal furnaces, and the soot settles into materials that were never designed to be cleaned. Katofsky Construction LLC answers calls 24/7 and works directly with the construction patterns common to this stretch of the Monongahela River valley, where steel-era rowhouses and duplexes sit close together and share wall assemblies that complicate containment.

## Why Homestead Properties See Fire Damage Spread Fast

Much of Homestead's housing stock dates back to the early 1900s, built quickly and densely for steelworkers and their families. Balloon-frame construction was common in that era, meaning the wall cavities run uninterrupted from basement to attic. A fire that starts in a kitchen or an electrical panel can travel vertically through those open stud bays far faster than it would in a modern platform-framed home. That same construction style also lets smoke and soot migrate into upper floors before flames ever reach them, which is why even a contained kitchen fire often leaves smoke damage throughout the house. Narrow lot lines and attached or near-attached structures common in this part of Allegheny County also mean a fire next door can push smoke into a home that never had flames in it at all.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Homestead

We start with board-up and tarping to secure the structure and stop weather or trespass from adding to the loss, which matters in Homestead's older homes where roof decking and window frames are already past their service life. From there we assess structural fire damage, separate what can be cleaned from what needs reconstruction, and move salvageable contents into restoration and storage before soot has time to etch into finishes. Smoke damage gets treated as its own phase, not an afterthought: ceilings, HVAC runs, and absorbent materials like older plaster and trim are tested and cleaned using methods matched to the surface, since plaster and original woodwork hold odor differently than drywall. Once the structure is stable and clean, our reconstruction and general contracting crews rebuild what was lost, matching period details where the home calls for it.

## Homestead Insurance Coordination

Fire claims in older Allegheny County housing often get complicated by code-upgrade requirements, since carriers frequently cover only like-kind repairs while local permitting may require electrical or structural work to meet current code. We document pre-loss conditions, structural fire damage, and smoke penetration in detail so the claim reflects what actually needs to happen, not just what burned. For duplexes and attached properties common in Homestead, we also document shared-wall exposure separately, since adjoining owners sometimes file through different carriers and the paperwork needs to stand on its own.

## Local note

A lot of Homestead's older homes still have knob-and-tube or early-generation wiring behind at least one wall, even after partial upgrades. When a fire originates in or near an electrical panel in one of these houses, we treat the surrounding wall cavities as compromised even if there's no visible char, because that older wiring runs through the same open stud bays that let smoke travel floor to floor. It's a detail that changes where we open walls for inspection, and it's specific to the building era this part of the Mon Valley was built in.
