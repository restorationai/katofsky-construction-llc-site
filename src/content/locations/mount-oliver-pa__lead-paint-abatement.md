**Lead paint abatement in Mount Oliver** usually starts with a question nobody wants to ask out loud: how old is this house, really? Mount Oliver is one of the older boroughs tucked into the hills around Pittsburgh, and a lot of its rowhouses and single-family frame homes went up long before 1978, when lead-based interior paint was still standard. If you're planning a renovation, selling a pre-war property, or just noticed chipping paint on a windowsill your kids can reach, the work has to be done under specific federal rules, not guesswork.

## Why Mount Oliver Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Mount Oliver's housing stock skews old. Much of the borough was built out in the early 1900s as a streetcar suburb for Pittsburgh's steel and rail workers, and that era of construction means layered paint, often a dozen coats deep, on trim, porches, and interior woodwork. Each layer below the newest coat has a real chance of containing lead, especially on exterior surfaces exposed to decades of sun and moisture cycling, which causes older paint to chalk and shed fine dust. Narrow lots and shared property lines common in the borough also mean that exterior scraping or sanding on one house can send dust toward a neighbor's yard if containment isn't handled correctly, which is part of why the EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting Rule exists in the first place.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process

We start with a visual condition assessment, looking at friction surfaces like windows and doors, impact points like stair treads, and any areas with visible deterioration. Work areas get sealed with poly sheeting and negative air containment before anything is disturbed, and we follow EPA RRP lead-safe work practices throughout, meaning wet methods instead of dry sanding, HEPA-filtered vacuums instead of shop vacs, and no open-flame paint removal. Debris is bagged and disposed of according to lead waste handling guidelines rather than tossed in a regular dumpster. Once the work is done, we clean the space with a structured wipe-down and visual check before the area is cleared for re-occupancy.

## Permitting Through Mount Oliver's Borough Office

One thing that trips up contractors unfamiliar with the area: Mount Oliver is its own incorporated borough, completely surrounded by the City of Pittsburgh but governed independently. That means renovation and demolition permits for a Mount Oliver address go through the borough's own code enforcement office, not Pittsburgh's Permits, Licenses, and Inspections department, even though the two are geographically seamless. We coordinate directly with the right office so there's no delay waiting on a permit application that was filed with the wrong municipality, which is a surprisingly common mix-up given how small the borough's footprint is.

## Local note

Mount Oliver's rowhouses were often built in attached blocks sharing a single roofline, which means exterior lead paint work on a porch or soffit rarely stays contained to just one address. Where units are attached, we walk the full block face before starting exterior abatement so containment barriers account for shared cornices and party walls, not just the property line on the deed.
