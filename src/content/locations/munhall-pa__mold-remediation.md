## Why Munhall Properties See Mold Issues

Munhall grew up as a steel town along the Monongahela, and a lot of its housing stock reflects that era: narrow lots, homes built into the hillside above the river, and foundations that were never engineered with modern vapor barriers in mind. Basements dug into that riverbank soil stay damp for weeks after a heavy rain, and older masonry foundations wick moisture straight through the block. Add in cast iron and galvanized plumbing that's reached the end of its service life in a lot of these homes, and a slow drain leak behind a wall can feed mold for months before anyone notices a stain. Western Pennsylvania's humid summers don't help either: when outdoor dew points climb, basements and first-floor crawlspaces in older Munhall homes can sit above the 60% relative humidity that lets common mold species establish within 24 to 48 hours of a moisture event.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Munhall

We start with a moisture map, not a guess. Our crew checks the source first, whether that's a sump pump that's been cycling overtime, a roof flashing failure, or a slow plumbing leak behind a hillside-facing wall, because remediation without fixing the source just means a return visit. From there we set up physical containment with negative air pressure, which matters more in these older Munhall homes than in newer construction because balloon-frame wall cavities can carry airflow (and spores) between floors in ways a modern stick-built house won't. Affected porous materials, drywall, insulation, carpet padding, get removed per IICRC S520 guidelines; salvageable framing and structural wood gets treated and dried rather than torn out, which keeps the job smaller and the cost down. We finish with HEPA air scrubbing and a final visual and moisture check before we close out containment.

## Local note

One thing we watch for specifically in Munhall: a lot of these riverside lots have detached garages or additions built directly into the hillside grade, sometimes decades after the original structure. Those additions often have their own, separate foundation drainage (or none at all), which means a mold problem in an attached addition can have a completely different moisture source than the main house, and treating them as one system usually means missing something. We test each structure's moisture separately before we scope the job.
