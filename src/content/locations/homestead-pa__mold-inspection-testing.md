**Wondering if that musty smell in your Homestead basement is something more than old house smell?** Mold inspection and testing matters here in a different way than in newer Pittsburgh-area suburbs: Homestead's housing stock dates largely to the steel mill era, and many of those century-old homes sit close to the Monongahela River on lots with limited grading. That combination of age and river proximity means moisture finds its way into foundations and crawlspaces more readily than it does in homes built on higher, drier ground.

## Why Homestead Properties See Mold Growth

Homestead grew up around the steel industry, and a large share of its housing was built quickly for mill workers in the early 1900s. Those homes often have shallow foundations, older masonry, and basements that sit close to the water table given the town's position along the Monongahela. When river humidity combines with aging mortar joints or a roof that's due for replacement, moisture gets into wall cavities and framing without an obvious leak ever showing up. Add in attics with poor ventilation, which is common in homes this age, and you get the slow, hidden colonization that homeowners usually don't notice until there's visible staining or a persistent odor. Mold doesn't need a flood to take hold here; it needs weeks of elevated humidity in a space nobody's checking on.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Homestead

We start with a visual walkthrough of the property, paying close attention to basements, crawlspaces, and any rooms that back up to exterior walls facing the river side of the property, since that's where we see the most moisture migration in this area. From there we map moisture levels room by room with a meter, noting any readings that run higher than what's typical for the home's age and construction. If conditions suggest active or hidden growth, we collect air samples to measure spore counts against an outdoor baseline, and we'll take surface samples from any suspect staining. Samples go to an independent lab for analysis, and we walk you through the results in plain language, not just a report full of spore genus names you have to Google.
