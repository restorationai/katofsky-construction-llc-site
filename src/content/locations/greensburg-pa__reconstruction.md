Rebuilding after a fire, a collapsed section of framing, or a tarped-off roof that failed before repairs could start is a different job than a routine remodel. In Greensburg, where much of the housing stock dates back to the early and mid-1900s, reconstruction work has to account for balloon framing, plaster-and-lath walls, and masonry that doesn't always match anything on the shelf at a supply house. Katofsky Construction LLC handles the structural rebuild phase after fire, smoke, or board-up damage, bringing the property back to a livable, code-compliant condition rather than leaving homeowners to manage a patchwork of subcontractors.

## Why Greensburg Properties Need Careful Reconstruction Planning

Westmoreland County's older housing stock, much of it built before modern fire-rated assemblies were standard, means a fire or structural failure often exposes original framing lumber, horsehair plaster, and knob-and-tube wiring remnants that complicate a straightforward rebuild. Western Pennsylvania's freeze-thaw winters also put stress on any structure left partially open after a fire or a bad storm event, which is why board-up and tarping work (something we handle directly) buys time but shouldn't be mistaken for a long-term fix. Reconstruction in Greensburg frequently means matching trim profiles and masonry coursing on a house built two or three generations ago, not just hanging new drywall.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Greensburg

We start with a structural walkthrough to separate what's salvageable from what needs to come out, documenting everything for the insurance file before demolition begins. From there, framing repairs and code-required upgrades (fire blocking, updated electrical runs, egress sizing) happen before any finish work starts, since covering a problem with new drywall only creates a second job later. Interior finishes, trim, and flooring are matched as closely as the original materials allow, and we coordinate permitting through the City of Greensburg building department as part of the project, not as a separate phone call the homeowner has to make. For fire-related rebuilds, this follows directly from our fire and smoke damage restoration work, so there's one point of contact from the day the fire department clears the scene through the final walkthrough.

## Insurance and Permitting Coordination

Most Greensburg rebuild projects run through a homeowner's insurance carrier, and the biggest delay we see isn't the construction itself, it's scope disagreements between an adjuster's initial estimate and what the structure actually needs once walls are opened. We document conditions with photos and written scope notes at each phase so supplemental claims have something concrete behind them. Where a property sits in a designated historic overlay or has an older structure that triggers additional review at the county level, we build that lead time into the schedule up front rather than letting it surprise anyone mid-project.

## Local note

One thing that catches homeowners off guard in Greensburg specifically: a lot of the brick construction downtown and in the surrounding older blocks uses a soft, lower-fired brick that doesn't take modern mortar well. Repointing or rebuilding a damaged masonry wall with a standard portland-cement mix can actually accelerate spalling over the following winter. We test mortar composition on older masonry before matching it, rather than assuming a generic mix will hold up through a Westmoreland County winter.
