When a kitchen fire, sewage backup, or storm-torn roof leaves a Monroeville home or business unusable, the cleanup crew and the rebuild crew need to be the same team, not two separate phone calls weeks apart. Katofsky Construction handles reconstruction services in Monroeville, PA from the structural framing stage through final finishes, so a damaged property moves toward a certificate of occupancy instead of sitting half-demolished while contractors are scheduled.

## Why Monroeville Properties Need Reconstruction Work

Monroeville grew up as a Pittsburgh-area suburb largely in the postwar decades, which means a lot of the housing stock is brick-veneer ranch and split-level construction from the 1950s through 1970s, mixed with newer commercial buildings along the borough's main corridors. Homes from that era often have balloon framing, plaster-and-lath walls in the original sections, and electrical or plumbing that's been updated piecemeal over seventy years. When fire, smoke, or sewage damage forces a partial teardown, matching new framing and finishes to what's already there takes more judgment than it does in a subdivision built in the last fifteen years. Western Pennsylvania's freeze-thaw winters also stress roofs and exterior walls that have already been compromised by fire or storm damage, which is why board-up and tarping work often has to happen fast to keep a structure from taking on additional moisture before reconstruction starts.

## Our Reconstruction Process in Monroeville

We start with a structural assessment that documents what burned, what got wet, and what's still sound, because insurance adjusters and Allegheny County code officials both want to see that distinction clearly before a permit gets pulled. From there we handle demolition of compromised materials, framing repair, subfloor and drywall replacement, and finish work, coordinating with our fire damage restoration and sewage cleanup and sanitization teams so the sequence runs cleanly: hazard mitigation first, structural rebuild second, finishes last. On older Monroeville homes we pay particular attention to matching trim profiles and plaster transitions where a partial wall replacement butts up against original 1960s-era construction, since mismatched texture is one of the most common complaints homeowners have after a rushed rebuild.

## Monroeville Insurance and Permit Coordination

Boroughs around Allegheny County each run their own permitting process, and Monroeville's building department expects the same documentation most municipalities do: scope of damage, structural drawings for anything load-bearing, and sign-off before drywall closes up new framing. We build our reconstruction timeline around those inspection points rather than skipping ahead and hoping it passes later, which is a mistake that costs homeowners weeks when a wall has to be opened back up. On the insurance side, we document damage with photos and a written scope before demolition begins, which gives adjusters something concrete to work from and helps prevent disputes over what was actually damaged versus what was already deteriorating before the loss.

## Local note

A detail that catches a lot of out-of-town contractors off guard: several of Monroeville's older residential streets sit on sloped lots graded for 1960s drainage standards, which means a fire or storm event that compromises a roof or gutter system can send water pooling against a foundation faster than it would on a flatter, newer lot. When we're scoping reconstruction after fire or storm damage on these properties, we check grading and drainage around the foundation as part of the structural assessment, not as an afterthought, because a rebuild that ignores it can end up with a new wall taking on moisture within the first wet season.
