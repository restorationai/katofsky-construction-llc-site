Rebuilding after a fire, a board-up emergency, or a biohazard cleanup is a different job in New Kensington than it is in a newer suburb. Much of the city's housing stock dates back to the early-to-mid 1900s, built quickly and densely for the workforce that once supported the aluminum plants along the Allegheny River. That means a lot of balloon-frame construction, plaster-and-lath walls, and additions that were never permitted the first time around. Katofsky Construction LLC handles the structural reconstruction phase after the damage is already assessed and the site is secured, working from Pittsburgh into Westmoreland County for homeowners who need a rebuild done right, not just fast.

## Why New Kensington Properties Need Careful Reconstruction

A house built in 1925 doesn't frame, insulate, or settle the way a 1998 build does. Many New Kensington homes sit on hillside lots with older stone or block foundations, and additions over the decades were often tied into the original structure in ways that aren't obvious until a wall is opened up. When fire damage restoration or biohazard cleanup clears a space down to the studs, we frequently find that the original framing has shifted, the electrical has been re-run multiple times by different owners, or the roofline doesn't match what's on file with the county. Reconstruction here means planning around what's actually behind the wall, not just what the blueprints say.

## Our Reconstruction Process After Fire or Damage Cleanup

Reconstruction starts once fire damage restoration, smoke damage restoration, or biohazard cleanup has finished and the space has been cleared for rebuild work. We document the pre-loss condition with photos and measurements, confirm scope with the adjuster, and pull the required permits through Westmoreland County before any framing goes back up. Structural repairs come first (joists, subfloor, load-bearing walls), followed by rough electrical and plumbing coordination, insulation, drywall, and finish work. On older New Kensington homes we budget extra time for matching trim profiles and plaster transitions so the repaired section doesn't read as an obvious patch next to the original walls.

## Insurance and Permitting Coordination

Most reconstruction jobs in New Kensington move through a homeowner's insurance claim, and we work directly with the adjuster to keep the scope of repair aligned with what was actually damaged versus what needs to be brought up to current code. Westmoreland County permitting can flag older additions that were never formally recorded, which sometimes adds a step before framing can start. We handle that documentation as part of the rebuild rather than leaving it for the homeowner to sort out mid-project.

## Local note

A lot of the city's older rowhouses and duplexes share a wall or a roofline with the property next door, which isn't something every contractor accounts for when framing a reconstruction. We check party-wall and setback conditions before demo starts on attached properties so the rebuild doesn't create a code issue with the neighboring structure, and so the finished line between old and new wall matches up cleanly instead of leaving a visible seam.
