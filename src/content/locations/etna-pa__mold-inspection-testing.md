Etna's housing stock sits close to Pine Creek and the Allegheny River, and that proximity shapes what we find when we open up a crawlspace or test indoor air here. Mold inspection and testing in a borough this low-lying isn't the same job as a hillside property in Fox Chapel or a newer build in Murrysville. The ground stays wetter longer, basements take on moisture through block walls that were never meant to handle it, and that's where spore counts climb before anyone notices a smell.

## Why Etna Properties See Mold Inspection Issues

Much of Etna's housing was built in the early-to-mid 1900s, when basements were framed with stone or block foundations and little to no vapor barrier. Add a borough with a documented history of flooding from Pine Creek, and you get a pattern we see again and again: moisture wicking up through foundation walls, condensation on cold basement pipes, and musty air that homeowners chalk up to "an old house smell" rather than active mold colonization. Once a flooring or drywall surface stays damp past 48 hours, conditions are right for growth, and in a tight rowhouse or duplex common to this part of Allegheny County, that growth can spread between units faster than it would in a detached suburban home.

## Our Mold Inspection and Testing Process in Etna

We start with a visual and moisture-meter walkthrough of the areas most likely to hold hidden dampness in an Etna home: basement sill plates, behind baseboard trim, and around any window or roof penetration. From there we take air samples and, where surface growth is visible, physical samples, sending both to an independent lab for species identification and spore-count analysis. Because older Etna homes often have additions or finished basements from different decades, we pay close attention to where building materials and eras meet, since that's typically where a vapor barrier was skipped or a seam was never sealed. Our technicians hold IICRC AMRT certification for mold assessment, and we document findings in a report format insurance adjusters and real estate inspectors can use directly.
