**Raw sewage in a Brentwood basement is not something to wait out.** Whether it is a sewer lateral backing up through a floor drain or a septic overflow after a heavy rain, category 3 water carries bacteria and pathogens that put your family or tenants at risk within hours. Katofsky Construction answers 24/7, and our crews are trained to contain, remove, and sanitize the affected areas so the property can be used again safely.

## Why Brentwood Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Brentwood's housing stock dates largely to the 1920s through the 1950s, and a lot of the borough's sewer laterals are original clay or cast iron pipe running downhill toward the municipal main. Clay pipe cracks and shifts with age, and tree roots find those cracks easily. Add in that much of Allegheny County still runs combined sanitary and stormwater sewers, a system ALCOSAN has been under a federal consent decree to overhaul for years, and you get a situation where a hard rain can push wastewater back into low-lying basements along Route 51 and the streets that drop toward it. Homes sited lower on Brentwood's hillside grades are especially exposed when the borough's lines surcharge faster than they drain.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Brentwood

We start by identifying the source, backup, overflow, or line failure, because that determines whether a plumber needs to clear a blockage before we can safely finish extraction. Standing sewage and saturated materials are removed first, with contaminated carpet, pad, and porous drywall typically cut out rather than cleaned, since category 3 water does not get treated as salvageable once it has soaked in. Hard surfaces, framing, and subfloor are then washed down with EPA-registered antimicrobial solutions and air-moved to dry per IICRC S500 guidelines. We document every step with photos for your insurance file, since sewage claims tend to draw closer scrutiny from carriers than a simple water loss.

## Local note

Brentwood's narrow lots and older foundations mean a lot of basements have a single floor drain tied directly into the lateral, with no backwater valve installed. On these properties, a backup often floods the lowest point in the house before anyone upstairs notices a problem, which is why we always check the floor drain and any sump pit first on an older Brentwood home, even if the visible damage is concentrated elsewhere.
