Homestead's housing stock dates mostly to the borough's steel-mill boom, when rowhouses and worker cottages went up fast along the hillsides above the Monongahela River. That means layer upon layer of old paint, much of it lead-based and applied well before the 1978 federal ban. Katofsky Construction handles lead paint abatement for these homes with the same containment discipline we'd use on a fire or biohazard job: isolate the area, control the dust, document the work.

## Why Homestead Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Much of Homestead's residential stock predates 1950, built quickly and cheaply to house steel workers and their families. Trim, porch rails, and interior doors in these homes often carry six or more coats of paint stacked over the decades, and the earliest layers are almost always lead-based. Add in the humidity that comes off the river valley, which causes older paint to chalk and flake faster than it would on higher, drier ground, and you get a steady stream of deteriorating paint on sills, porches, and stair treads, exactly the surfaces kids and pets touch most.

Renovation and demolition work on these properties also disturbs paint that's been stable for years. Pulling out a 1930s window casing or sanding a porch floor for repaint can release more lead dust in an afternoon than decades of normal wear.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Homestead

We start with a visual assessment and sampling of suspect surfaces, paying close attention to areas with chipping, friction (windows, doors), or impact damage (baseboards, stair treads). On Homestead's tighter rowhouse lots, containment setup usually has to account for shared walls and narrow side yards, so we build negative-air enclosures sized to the actual workspace rather than a generic room template.

Abatement itself follows lead-safe work practices consistent with EPA Renovation, Repair, and Painting (RRP) guidelines: wet methods to limit airborne dust, HEPA vacuum cleanup, and plastic sheeting sealed at every transition point. Debris is bagged and disposed of as regulated waste, not thrown in with general construction trash. Once the work area is clean, we do a final visual check and wipe sampling before clearing the space for re-occupancy or the next phase of renovation.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Lead Paint

HEPA-filtered vacuums and air scrubbers do most of the heavy lifting on dust control, paired with wet scraping or chemical stripping rather than dry sanding wherever surface condition allows. For components we're not removing outright, encapsulation with a lead-rated coating is sometimes the more practical option, especially on ornate millwork common to Homestead's older homes that would be costly to replace. We choose the method based on the substrate, the condition of the paint, and whether the homeowner plans to repaint or replace the material afterward.
