Reconstruction after a fire, a sewage event, or a failed roof means different things depending on what's underneath the drywall, and in Homestead that's often balloon-framed lumber and knob-and-tube wiring remnants from the borough's mill-worker housing boom. Katofsky Construction handles the structural rebuild from framing to finish once the damage is documented and the site is safe, working inside Allegheny County's permitting process rather than around it.

## Why Homestead Properties See Reconstruction Needs

Homestead grew up fast around the Homestead Steel Works, and a lot of the borough's housing stock dates to that era: narrow-lot rowhouses and duplexes built close together, often sharing party walls or tight side yards. That construction pattern means fire and smoke damage frequently travels further than it would in a modern subdivision, because older wall cavities aren't firestopped the way current code requires. Add in decades of electrical and plumbing patchwork, and a single-room fire can turn into a reconstruction job that touches several rooms once we open the walls and see what the framing actually looks like. The Monongahela River's humidity doesn't help either, since saturated framing from firefighting water or a failed roof takes longer to dry out before rebuild can start.

## Our Reconstruction Services Process in Homestead

We start with a structural assessment to separate what's salvageable from what has to come out, which in pre-1940s Homestead homes often includes checking for lead paint on original trim and plaster, and asbestos in older floor tile or pipe wrap before any demolition begins. Once hazardous materials are cleared or abated, we move into framing repair, subfloor and drywall replacement, and finish carpentry, coordinating fire damage restoration and smoke damage restoration work so odor and soot don't get sealed behind new material. For jobs where the roof structure itself was compromised, we sequence emergency board-up or tarping first to keep the site weathertight while the rebuild scope is finalized. Every phase is documented with photos and material specs, which matters later when your adjuster is reviewing the claim.

## Homestead Insurance & Permit Coordination

Borough permitting in Homestead runs through Allegheny County's building code enforcement for most structural work, and older homes sometimes trigger additional review if the original framing doesn't meet current span or fire-rating requirements. We pull the necessary permits as part of the reconstruction scope rather than leaving that step to the homeowner, and we provide the itemized documentation most carriers want to see before releasing funds for a full rebuild versus a patch repair. If your policy language is unclear on replacement cost versus actual cash value for structural work, we flag that early so it doesn't stall the job mid-project.

## Local note

A lot of Homestead lots were platted in the 1890s when the borough was built out around the steel works, which means narrow frontages and tight side-yard access that weren't designed with dumpsters, material staging, or lift equipment in mind. On these addresses we plan delivery and debris removal around the lot's actual width rather than assuming standard clearance, which keeps the job moving instead of stalling on the first day over where the dumpster can even sit.
