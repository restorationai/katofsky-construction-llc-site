Bellevue's rowhouses and duplexes along California Avenue date mostly to the streetcar-suburb boom of the early 1900s, which means a lot of this borough's trim, siding, and interior woodwork still carries layers of lead-based paint under newer coats. Katofsky Construction LLC handles lead paint abatement for Bellevue homeowners and property managers who are renovating, selling, or just trying to make an older house safe for young kids, and we scope every job around what's actually under that paint, not a guess.

## Why Bellevue Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Bellevue was built out fast between roughly 1900 and the 1930s, when lead-based paint was standard for both exterior clapboard and interior trim. Walk through the borough today and you'll see a lot of original window sashes, porch columns, and baseboards that have never been fully stripped, just painted over. Add in Allegheny County's humid summers and freeze-thaw winters, both of which stress exterior paint films and accelerate chalking and chipping, and you get a steady supply of deteriorating paint on sills, soffits, and porch rails, exactly the surfaces where dust and paint chips end up in soil and window wells. Any renovation that disturbs those layers (sanding a sash, pulling trim, cutting into a wall near the Ohio River-facing side of a house) can release lead dust into the work area if it isn't contained first.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Bellevue

We start with a paint condition assessment before anything gets touched, since not every painted surface in a Bellevue rowhouse needs full abatement; some just need stabilization and encapsulation. Where removal is warranted, we follow lead-safe work practices under the EPA's Renovation, Repair, and Painting rule: containment of the work area with poly sheeting, wet methods instead of dry sanding, HEPA-filtered vacuums for cleanup, and verification cleaning before we call a room finished. For exterior work on Bellevue's narrow lots, that containment often has to account for how close the next house sits, since dust drift onto a neighbor's property or shared walkway is a real risk on these tight streetcar-suburb blocks. We bag and dispose of lead debris according to Pennsylvania's hazardous waste handling requirements rather than putting it in a standard dumpster.

## Local Note

A lot of Bellevue's porch columns and window sashes were replaced or patched piecemeal over the decades, so it's common to find three or four different paint eras stacked on a single piece of trim, some lead-based, some not. We test layer by layer rather than assuming the whole board is contaminated, which keeps the scope (and the cost) focused on what actually needs abatement instead of stripping trim that doesn't require it.
