## Why Bellevue Properties See Sewage Backup Issues

Bellevue is a dense, hilly borough wedged between the Ohio River and the ridge toward Avalon, and most of its housing stock predates 1950. That means clay tile laterals, cast iron stacks, and sewer mains that were never sized for the stormwater volume a modern downpour produces. Allegheny County's regional treatment authority (ALCOSAN) manages a largely combined sewer system across this part of the North Hills, so sanitary waste and stormwater share the same pipe. During a sustained rain event, that combined flow backs up fastest at the lowest point in a house, which in Bellevue's older rowhouses and duplexes is almost always the basement. Add in septic or private lateral lines on some of the borough's outlying parcels, and you get overflow patterns that look different house to house even on the same block.

## Our Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization Process in Bellevue

We start by classifying the water. Anything from a sewer backup or septic overflow is treated as Category 3, meaning porous materials it has contacted, carpet pad, baseboard, drywall below the flood line, generally get removed rather than dried in place, following the IICRC S500 standard for contaminated water losses. In Bellevue's older homes we're often dealing with plaster-and-lath basement ceilings, dirt-floor crawlspaces under additions, and galvanized plumbing that complicates where the contamination actually traveled. We extract standing water, remove affected materials, apply EPA-registered antimicrobial sanitizing agents to framing and subfloor, and run negative air and dehumidification to bring the space back to a safe moisture level before closing anything up. Containment barriers go up early, since a sewage event in a finished basement with HVAC returns nearby can spread odor and contamination to the rest of the house fast.

## Bellevue Insurance Coordination

Sewage losses get scrutinized differently than a clean water claim, since many homeowner policies carry a separate sewer backup rider with its own limit. We document the source of the backup, whether it traces to the municipal main or a private lateral, photograph every affected material before removal, and provide the itemized scope adjusters expect for a contaminated-water claim. If your policy doesn't carry sewer backup coverage, or the limit is thin, we'll tell you that up front rather than after the work starts, so there are no surprises on the invoice.
