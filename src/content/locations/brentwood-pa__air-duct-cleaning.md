If your furnace kicks on in a Brentwood rowhouse and the air coming out smells faintly like a basement that never quite dries, the ductwork is usually the reason, not the furnace itself. Brentwood's housing stock, dense blocks of 1920s through 1950s duplexes and bungalows built into Allegheny County's hillsides, still runs a lot of original galvanized sheet metal trunk lines, and those systems hold decades of dust, pet dander, and in some cases old coal-furnace soot that never fully cleared out when homes converted to gas or electric heat.

## Why Brentwood Properties See Air Duct Buildup

Brentwood sits in a tight grid of narrow lots just south of Pittsburgh, and a lot of the borough's homes were built before central air conditioning was standard, meaning ductwork was often added or retrofitted later, sometimes squeezed through crawlspaces and partial basements that run damp in spring. That dampness matters: humid duct interiors trap dust instead of letting it pass through, and homes near the lower-lying streets close to Streets Run tend to run a few degrees more humid than homes higher up the hill. Add in the number of older two-family conversions in the borough, where one HVAC system sometimes serves ductwork that was extended into a later addition, and you get systems with uneven airflow and buildup concentrated in the sections furthest from the furnace.

## Our Air Duct Cleaning Process in Brentwood

We start by inspecting the full run, supply and return, with a camera where the layout allows it, because in a lot of Brentwood's older homes the duct path isn't the one shown on any blueprint, additions and converted attics tend to reroute things over the years. From there we use negative-air containment at the main trunk so dislodged debris doesn't just redistribute into the living space, then agitate and vacuum each branch run back to the registers. Dryer vent lines get the same attention on most calls here, since many Brentwood homes have dryer runs that exit through an exterior wall rather than straight up through the roof, which creates more bends, more lint buildup, and more fire risk than a vertical run.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Air Duct

We use rotary brush agitation paired with high-powered vacuum extraction at the source, which is a better match for older galvanized trunk lines than purely negative-pressure systems, since rigid metal duct doesn't flex the way newer flexible duct does and needs mechanical agitation to break buildup loose. Supply registers, return grilles, and the plenum itself get cleaned as part of the same visit, and we sanitize surfaces inside the system using products rated for HVAC interiors rather than general-purpose cleaners. For homes with dryer vent runs longer than a typical straight shot, we use flexible rotary cabling to clear lint the full length of the duct, not just what's visible from the exterior termination point.

## Local note

A number of Brentwood's older duplexes were split from single-family homes decades ago, and the duct modifications made at that time weren't always sealed properly at the branch takeoffs. We often find these gaps pulling basement or crawlspace air directly into the supply stream, which means a thorough duct cleaning in these homes also includes resealing accessible joints, not just clearing debris, otherwise the system re-contaminates itself within a season.
