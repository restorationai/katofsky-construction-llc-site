## Restoration emergencies common in Sharpsburg

Sharpsburg sits in the Allegheny River's floodplain, and its age shows in the housing stock: narrow lots, older brick and frame rowhouses, and basements that were built for a different era of plumbing and drainage. When the river rises or a storm drain backs up, water finds its way into these basements fast, and it tends to sit there because the original foundations and drains weren't designed for today's rainfall volumes. Add Pittsburgh's freeze-thaw winters, where pipes in uninsulated crawlspaces and older additions are prone to splitting, and you get a steady pattern of burst-pipe and basement-flooding calls alongside the seasonal storm surges. Older wiring and aging furnaces in homes from this era also raise the odds of fire and smoke damage, particularly in houses that haven't been fully updated.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Sharpsburg

Much of Sharpsburg was built as a river-mill town in the late 1800s and early 1900s, which means brick and wood-frame rowhouses with masonry or rubble-stone foundations and, in many cases, galvanized or cast-iron plumbing that's now well past its service life. Basements are common rather than slab or crawlspace construction, and that combination of old masonry and old pipe is exactly where we see the slow leaks and sudden pipe failures that trigger a restoration call. Homes from this era can also contain asbestos-containing materials in old flooring, insulation, or pipe wrap, and lead paint on original trim, both of which change how a demolition scope has to be planned and documented. Low-lying stretches near the Allegheny River are mapped into FEMA flood zones in parts of the borough, which can affect rebuild requirements after a flood loss. Structural repair and rebuild work is permitted through the borough under Pennsylvania's Uniform Construction Code, and any load-bearing or electrical work typically needs a permit before it starts.
