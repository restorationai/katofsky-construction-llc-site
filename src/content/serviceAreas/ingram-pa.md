## Restoration emergencies common in Ingram

Ingram's position along Chartiers Creek means the borough has a real history with high water, and basements here take on moisture even in years without a named storm, just from heavy rain running off the surrounding hillsides faster than older storm infrastructure can carry it. A lot of the housing stock was built in the early-to-mid 20th century as a streetcar suburb of Pittsburgh, so you've got a mix of frame homes and brick rowhouses with full basements, galvanized or early copper supply lines, and knob-and-tube wiring in some unrenovated units. Combine aging plumbing with a valley floor prone to standing water and you get the two calls we see most from Ingram: basement flooding after sustained rain, and slow leaks behind walls or under old bathroom tile that go unnoticed until the drywall or plaster starts to bow. Winter freeze-thaw cycles add a third pattern, pipes that crack in uninsulated crawlspaces or exterior-wall runs during a hard cold snap.
